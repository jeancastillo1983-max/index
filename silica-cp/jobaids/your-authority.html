Silica Competent Person · Job aid · Module 2

Your authority in one page

You can spot it. You are allowed to fix it. Here is what that looks like on a Tuesday.

What makes you the competent person

Capable of identifying existing and foreseeable respirable crystalline silica hazards, and authorized by your employer to take prompt corrective measures to eliminate or minimize them. You also need the knowledge to implement the written exposure control plan. 1926.1153(b)

Your named duty

Make frequent and regular inspections of job sites, materials and equipment to implement the written exposure control plan. 1926.1153(g)(4)

You can stop it and fix it

When you seeDo this now
Dry cutting, grinding, drilling or chipping with no controlHold the work. Get water, shroud or vac working. Restart only when dust is controlled.
A control that is there but not working (dry tank, clogged filter, cracked shroud, low airflow)Hold the work until it is fixed. A half-working control is not a Table 1 control.
Wrong respirator for the hours or the location, a beard under a seal, no fit testSwap the respirator or the worker before the task starts.
Dry sweeping, or the air hose used for cleanupHEPA vac or wet sweep. Put the broom and the hose away.
Other trades, including other employers, in your dustStop the source, move people out, barrier and sign. Then talk to their foreman.
A task that is not in the plan or not on Table 1Hold it. Call the safety manager for controls and an exposure assessment.

Say it like this

"Hold the cut a minute. The water is not reaching the blade. Fill the tank and we are back on in five."
"I hear you on the schedule. Here is how we keep it and keep the vac on."

Tell, and write it down

Tell your supervisor or the super what you stopped and why. Write the time, what you found and what fixed it on your daily checklist. If it keeps happening, it is a plan problem: tell the safety manager.
